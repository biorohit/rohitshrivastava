Fellowship applications / Personal experience

EMBO Long-Term Fellowship

By Rohit Shrivastava · 5 min read

Applying for EMBO long term fellowship

In ever competitive field of science, a 2 year post doc is not sufficient to take research to a logical conclusion which can give fruitful results and to publish in high impact journals. Publishing in high impact journals is a necessary evil in academic science. Also, having lost 2 months to lockdown, 2 months of limited working and another month to training and slow delivery of consumables, I had to somehow extend my stay in my current lab. Although there were news that we all will get extension from 3 months to 1 year on case to case basis. However, eventually it was clear only researcher whose career was at risk will get priority. So, I had to look for external fellowships.

Since I was already in France and working in the institute I was late to apply for Human Frontiers Science Programme (HFSP). Other fellowships left to apply were EMBO Long term fellowship and Marie Skłodowska-Curie Individual fellowship.

Firstly, I planned to apply for EMBO long term fellowship (link below) as the eligibility requires the applicant shouldn’t have been working in the host lab for more than 6 months. EMBO application can be submitted anytime during the year but they have two annual deadlines. Applications submitted after that will be evaluated in next call. Since, I was about to compete 6 months in the lab on 1st August so I had to submit application to EMBO by end July.

Although the EMBO FAQ and online form was straight forward to fill. It required a general information about me. Followed by two referees of which 1 should be PhD supervisor. Suggesting a referee is always challenging as some people might not have time or will they give a strong references. So I took chances to include a member from my PhD committee. The host institute PI also got an email to submit a letter of support, my PI wrote a very strong letter of support for me. Now comes the serious stuff –

  1. in 200 words “describe your personal motivation to choose this particular laboratory for your postdoctoral training”. This was the crux of the application. My PI helped me to frame it in the best possible way. This is the place to write how I’m best for this project, how the host lab/institute will diversify my skills and the scientific excellence contributing to solving a problem.
  2. Next was a 250 word “Summary and biological significance of the proposed research”. This was the most crucial as it should highlight the scientific excellence and importance of the planned work.
  3. The biggest section was to describe the “Proposed work at the receiving institute” in 1500 words. This section was to describe the introduction, preliminary data, scientific objectives and experimental approach for the proposed work. Since space is limited its very difficult to include all the information plus the future scope of the proposed work. Only plus point was references had to be included in another section saving space.
  4. Next was to suggest keywords, subject areas, starting date of fellowship, duration and interviewers conflicts and interviewers suggestions.
  5. Details of host institute, followed by work done at the home institute in 500 words.
  6. PhD details – award date, country, duration, name and institute of supervisor.
  7. Next was in 200 words to “describe briefly the main scientific achievements of your PhD project”. This is the place to highlight that you have been part of something scientifically challenging and have capacity to solve problems and lead the project.
  8. Next was other information, basically information which generally goes into the CV. As there is no CV to upload here so information have to be included in relevant sub-sections. Here in information related to “grants, awards, contributions or fellowships (towards either travel or subsistence expenses of the proposed visit) you are receiving or applying for”. Here I included travel grants that I received.
  9. Next, “List previously held fellowships from EMBO or other organisations with dates (month/year)”. Here I listed fellowships that I received for PhD and postdoc.
  10. Next section was to include time taken off – Career Break and reasons for it.
  11. Next section was basically the break down of CV including “Research experience since PhD” and “Education and training prior to PhD work, starting from the beginning of your university”.
  12. Next section was on publications – number of peer-reviewed articles and number of first author peer-reviewed articles. List three primary research papers which in your view provided the most important and original contributions to scientific knowledge and List any additional (co-) authored, peer-reviewed publications in international journals. List your (co-) authored publications belonging to the category of review articles, viewpoints, etc. The problem with EMBO is they don’t consider manuscripts under review or submitted ones. In addition, I also added 2-3 lines detailing my contribution below each paper.
  13. Last was to list prizes and honours received.

The biggest problem I faced was that there was no reference application to follow. We got the proposed work example from someone from our institute so I had to opportunity to follow the same structure. But in general its a big bottleneck for future applicants.

The positive thing from this experience was I got better understanding of the literature on the subject and better understood the experimental plan and objectives to achieve the goal. Certainly this experience helped me to gain expertise in formulation of a research project, experimental approached to achieve the goals and in writing skills.
