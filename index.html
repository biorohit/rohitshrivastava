I study how failures in RNA and Protein Quality Control drive disease, from molecular mechanisms to patient-derived neural models. RESEARCH NARRATIVE

One question.

Three research chapters.

Following one mechanistic question through three countries and three disease systems: how does a cell's RNA and protein quality control machinery fail under stress, and what happens to surrounding tissue when it collapses?

Phase I — Single-Cell Stress Response PhD in Israel

Stress Granule Assembly & Virulence

During my PhD at Ben-Gurion University, I uncovered translation-independent roles of the cap-binding protein eIF4E-3 in stress granule assembly and virulence in Leishmania. This established that stress granule components carry specific disease-relevant biological functions beyond their standard textbook roles in translation.

Phase II — Cell-Autonomous Intervention Postdoc in France

Therapeutic Validation in ALS Proteostasis

Transitioned to human pathology at CRBM-CNRS, co-leading the discovery and validation of NEDP1-targeting nanobodies. We engineered CNS-targeted delivery via botulinum constructs to improve CNS target engagement in ALS models, moving from an invertebrate pathogen response directly to a therapeutically viable target.

Phase III — Intercellular Disease Spread MSCA in Poland

Non-Cell-Autonomous Extracellular Vesicle Propagation

My current MSCA fellowship project (AstroExo-HD) asks whether failing quality control (proteostasis collapse) in astrocytes is packaged into extracellular vesicles and exported to intact neurons, propagating Huntington's disease pathology non-cell-autonomously.

CURRENT PROJECT: AstroExo-HD · WORKING MODEL

Is degeneration something a neuron suffers — or something it catches?

The working model behind current MSCA-funded work: that Huntington's disease astrocytes export vesicles that propagate proteostasis collapse to neurons, making pathology an intercellular process that could be intercepted at the messenger.
